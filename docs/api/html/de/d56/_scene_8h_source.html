65/// @class Scene Scene.h <pbr/Scene.h>
66/// @brief The Scene class handles an abstraction of the scene:
67/// - info on the camera
68/// - the geometry and how to intersect it and get visibility or shadow intersections
69/// - the lights in the scene (for now we only support a single layer lightmask)
70/// - the camera
71///
72/// COORDINATE SYSTEMS IN USE:
73/// --------------------------
74///
75/// - Render space (R) is the coordinate system in which we generate geometry,
76/// build our intersection acceleration structure (BVH), trace rays, execute
77/// integrators, Bsdf and Bssrdf closures, lights, shading system, shaders.
78/// Basically everything that happens during rendering happens in render
79/// space. It's usually a space that's more convenient or less likely to have
80/// floating point precision issues than world space. Currently, render space
81/// is defined to be the same as camera space when we first begin rendering
82/// (at shutter-open / t=0 in case the camera transform is animated).
83/// However, the camera may move between frames in an interactive application,
84/// so we must divorce camera space from render space to avoid rebuilding the
85/// BVH (among other things) whenever the camera moves.
86///
87/// - Camera space (C) is the coordinate system where the origin is at the focal
88/// point of the camera and the camera looks down the -Z axis (X points to the
89/// right and Y points up). This coordinate system is also unlikely to cause
90/// floating point precision issues, and is arguably convenient when debugging.
91/// It is used only to initialize render space when the first render starts.
92///
93/// - World space (W) is world-space. Not much to say about that, except we
94/// don't ever use it explicitly, and especially not in single floating point
95/// precision, otherwise that's when trouble happens when the scene is far from
96/// the origin. We may use it in some transforms to build matrices that go from
97/// one space to another. You may need to use it explicitly to convert input
98/// data that is in world space, but be careful to use double precision in that
99/// case (convert to render space, then down cast to float).
100///
101/// In this library most interfaces deal with rays and intersection
102/// data-structures in render space.
103
