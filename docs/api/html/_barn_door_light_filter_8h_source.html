12// The Barn Door light filter is based on the on set technique of hinging doors
13// to light fixtures to control and shape and spill of light. As it simulates
14// being a light attachment, it works best with positional lights i.e. NOT
15// directional and env lights. There are two modes to this filter; physical and
16// analytic. Physical giving us a more accurate representation of real shadows,
17// looking at the size of the light, the distance of the barn door to the
18// subject etc. Analytic skews results in favour of artistic control over
19// physical accuracy.
20//
21// The user configures a rectangular portal which is defined in a similar
22// fashion as the CookieLightFilter. There is a projector which has a position
23// and direction and projects essentially a procedural texture: a blurry
24// rectangle. The blurry rectangle represents the portal defined by the ends of
25// the four flaps of a real barn door, here known as the "flap opening". It can
26// have rounded corners, blurry edges, and be resized (as if adjusting the angle
27// of the virtual flaps). The focal distance determines how far away the portal
28// sits from the filter origin.
29//
30// In analytic mode we use the projection of the shading point directly into
31// screen space.
32//
33// For physical mode we intersect the light ray with the plane of the
34// rectangular portal, then project that point into screen space.
35//
36// Once the point is in the screen space, it is checked to see if its within the
37// (rounded, blurry) rectangle.
